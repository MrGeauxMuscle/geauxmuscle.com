How it counts

Three counts have to agree before a set is yours.

Most apps count once and hope. GeauxMuscle counts on the wrist, counts again on video, and asks the one person who was there.

1 · Watch

Listens on the wrist

Motion sensors on your Apple Watch pick out each rep and the speed of the bar. Nothing is tapped mid-set.

Listening… · counting starts after a few reps
2 · iPhone

Re-counts from video

Prop the phone up and it tracks your body through the set. You tap yourself once so it never follows your spotter.

Confirms, or corrects

The app says what it thinks and asks. "Confirm" or "Not quite". Your count is the one that gets the seal.

Honest by design

It says what it knows, and what it doesn't.

The rules the app is written to, in the words it uses on screen.

No lit zero

The count stays dim until the first rep lands. You will never see a glowing "0" pretending to be a measurement.

Doubt out loud

"I can't tell what movement this was yet." When it isn't sure, it tells you, instead of guessing and moving on.

Your count wins

The app's number is a proposal. Yours gets the checkmark seal, and only confirmed sets go in the log.

Amber means look

A number that can't be trusted shows in amber with the reason. Never as fact, never as a red-amber-green verdict.

Short sets

Under three reps, counting isn't reliable yet, and the app says so on the set.

On device

Reps, video and watch data stay on your watch and phone. There is no GeauxMuscle server, so there is nothing to upload.
